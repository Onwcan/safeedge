OPC UA subscription latency: runtime decision -> client notification
====================================================================

Three processes: safeedged decides and publishes a snapshot to shared
memory, safeedge-opcua serves it, and a subscribed client times its own
notifications against the transition instant the runtime stamped.

The first notification of each run is discarded: a subscription opens by
reporting the current value, which is state rather than an event.

  publish  10 ms  sampling  10 ms   n=6   min 7.2 ms   median 10.1 ms   max 13.2 ms
  publish  10 ms  sampling  50 ms   n=6   min 15.4 ms   median 30.9 ms   max 52.1 ms
  publish  10 ms  sampling 200 ms   n=6   min 163.1 ms   median 180.4 ms   max 194.8 ms
  publish 100 ms  sampling  10 ms   n=6   min 15.1 ms   median 25.5 ms   max 38.7 ms

What binds this is the subscription's REVISED publishing interval, which
is the server's decision and not the client's. With open62541's stock
limits a client asking for 10 ms is given 100 ms publishing and 50 ms
sampling, and reports a median near 75 ms; the probe prints the revision
so the difference is visible rather than assumed.

safeedge-opcua lowers publishingIntervalLimits.min and
samplingIntervalLimits.min to 10 ms, so the same request is granted and
the same client reports a median near 12 ms. Six times, from a server
setting -- the client never had the say.

A subscription is therefore not inherently faster than a poll. It removes
the CLIENT's poll. The server-side intervals remain, and they are
somebody's configuration rather than a property of the protocol.
